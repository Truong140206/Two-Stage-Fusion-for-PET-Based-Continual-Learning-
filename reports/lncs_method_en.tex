\begin{abstract}
Parameter-efficient continual learners maintain task-specific adapters whose
selection affects both routing and classification. We augment HRM-PET with a
random-projection (RP) head fitted by ridge regression over fixed first-task
LoRA features. Its sufficient statistics are accumulated
task by task without retaining examples. The same RP scores support two
inference-time operations: task-level fusion with the initial adapted prediction
before re-matching, and class-level fusion with the final logits using a
top-two-margin gate. Across three benchmarks and four seeds, DRM-proposal
accuracy increases by $1.10$--$2.26$ points and classification accuracy by
$0.69$--$1.50$, with no consistent improvement in forgetting or backward
transfer. Single-seed ablations across five backbones show that the routing
contribution depends on the gate. A four-seed comparison against ungated
class-only fusion favours the full method on ImageNet-R and CIFAR-100, but
does not resolve a difference on CUB-200; this comparison measures the joint
addition of routing and gating. A grid over the two mixing weights shows that
classification accuracy is nearly flat in the routing weight, so the reported
value is not a resolved optimum.

\keywords{Continual learning \and Parameter-efficient tuning \and Parameter
routing \and Random projection \and Signal fusion.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Continual learning asks a model to absorb a stream of tasks without losing what
earlier ones taught it. The dominant recent answer freezes a large pre-trained
backbone and attaches a small set of task-specific parameters --- prompts,
adapters, low-rank updates --- so that a new task writes into fresh capacity
instead of overwriting old weights. Old parameters survive and no exemplars need
be stored, which is why parameter-efficient tuning now leads the exemplar-free
setting~\cite{ref_l2p,ref_dualprompt,ref_hide}. Keeping the old parameters is
not the same as keeping the old accuracy, which is what the metrics measure.

The design moves the difficulty rather than removing it. At inference the task
identity is unknown, so before the model can classify it must choose which
parameter set to attach. That choice is itself a prediction, and every later
computation inherits it: attach the wrong adapter and the classifier is asked to
work from features it was never adapted for. Successive methods have improved
the choice --- larger pools, attention-weighted mixtures, explicit re-matching
stages that revisit an initial guess --- but the choice still rests on evidence
drawn from one place, the backbone's own predictions.

We ask what a second, differently derived source of evidence would be worth, and
find one available for the price of a forward pass: the features such a learner
computes anyway, pushed through a frozen random projection and read by a ridge
classifier fitted in closed form from accumulated second-order statistics, in
the manner of RanPAC~\cite{ref_ranpac}. That head stores no examples, changes no
training procedure, and --- the property that matters here --- makes different
mistakes from the routing module. We reuse it at the two points where the
pipeline commits: to revise the task proposal before re-matching, and to blend
with the final class logits under a per-sample weight that grows as the primary
classifier's top-two margin narrows. Taking HRM-PET~\cite{ref_hrmpet} as the
pipeline to build on, we measure what each operation contributes, and say so
where the evidence does not settle the question.

\subsubsection{Contributions.}
We examine unused complementary evidence in HRM-PET~\cite{ref_hrmpet} and show
that a better task proposal does not automatically become a better class
prediction, because the shared head frequently predicts the correct class in
spite of a wrong route (Sect.~\ref{sec:baseline}). We show that a second-order
head over a frozen random projection of the existing features, fitted in closed
form and never by gradient descent, supplies evidence with partially different
errors, and we fuse the two at the routing and the classification level alike,
entirely at inference time. We evaluate on three datasets over four seeds and
report by axis: routing improves by $+1.10$ to $+2.26$ points and classification
by $+0.69$ to $+1.50$, each several times the seed-to-seed standard deviation,
while the two retention metrics show no consistent improvement
(Sect.~\ref{sec:exp}). We reconstruct the full grid the original work covers ---
four datasets by five pre-training configurations, identified from traces in its
released code --- where all $20$ cells improve on both accuracy axes, over
baselines spanning $31.8$ to $93.8$ Acc@1 (Sect.~\ref{sec:grid}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsubsection{Parameter pools and the selection they require.}
L2P~\cite{ref_l2p} and DualPrompt~\cite{ref_dualprompt} set the shape most
parameter-efficient continual learners still follow: freeze the backbone, keep a
pool of lightweight parameter sets, and pick from it at inference using a query
from the bare backbone. CODA-Prompt~\cite{ref_coda} replaces the discrete pick
with an attention-weighted combination; HiDe-Prompt~\cite{ref_hide} separates
within-task prediction, task-identity inference and task-adaptive prediction and
optimises them apart, observing that identity inference, not within-task
classification, is what limits the pipeline. HRM-PET~\cite{ref_hrmpet}, our
baseline, substitutes LoRA adapters~\cite{ref_lora} for prompts and adds
re-matching stages that revisit the initial decision. Later work enlarges the
expert family: MoTE~\cite{ref_mote} combines task-specific experts, and
DLEPEM~\cite{ref_dlepem} combines general and adapted prototypes for matching.

\subsubsection{Closed-form solutions in continual learning.}
A separate line replaces gradient descent on the classifier with a solution
written in closed form, which removes the mechanism by which sequential updates
overwrite one another. Nearest-class-mean prototypes are the simplest instance,
and APER~\cite{ref_adam} adapts the backbone on the first task only, then reads
frozen features. Analytic continual learning states the problem as least squares
directly: ACIL~\cite{ref_acil} recasts class-incremental learning as a recursive
least-squares problem in which a correlation matrix rather than a sample buffer
carries the past, yielding a model equal to the one a joint fit would produce,
and DS-AL~\cite{ref_dsal} adds a second stream to recover nonlinear capacity
that a single linear solve gives up. RanPAC~\cite{ref_ranpac} reaches the same
form from the feature side: frozen features through a fixed random projection,
second-order statistics accumulated across tasks, one ridge system solved.

Our head belongs to this family and we claim no novelty in it: the projection,
the Gram accumulation and the ridge solve are RanPAC's. What differs is its
role. These methods use the closed-form classifier as the whole predictor and
discard routing; we keep the routing pipeline and consult the solved head twice.

\subsubsection{Correcting the classifier after the fact.}
Class-incremental classifiers lean towards recent classes, and the survey of
Masana et al.~\cite{ref_survey} locates the bias in both the bias terms and the
classifier weight norms. Corrections follow: iCaRL~\cite{ref_icarl} classifies
to the nearest exemplar mean; BiC~\cite{ref_bic} learns a per-task affine map on
the logits from a held-out split; Weight Aligning~\cite{ref_wa} rescales
new-class weight norms to match the old; LUCIR~\cite{ref_lucir} normalises
features and weights before the softmax. We keep the adapters, auxiliary
classifier and shared head fixed and retain no exemplars, though current-task
data are still needed to fit the RP head, whose scores we fuse at inference at a
cost included in the reported total.

\subsection{HRM-PET: The Baseline We Build On}
\label{sec:baseline}

We use the released HRM-PET~\cite{ref_hrmpet} inference path. Diagnostics quoted
here are seed-$42$ measurements, separate from the evaluation of Sect.~\ref{sec:exp}.

\subsubsection{Task-identity inference (TII).}
The input image is passed through the \emph{bare} backbone, with no adapter
attached, to obtain a query feature $q(x) = \phi(x;\theta_{\mathrm{ptm}})$,
writing $\phi$ for the backbone under whatever adapter it is given. That
feature enters an auxiliary classifier trained with cross-entropy, which gives
unnormalised class logits $u = g(q(x); \theta_\omega)$. The initial match is
then $t_0=\mathcal T(\arg\max_c u_c)$.

\subsubsection{Re-matching (DRM and CRM).}
The first adapted pass attaches $p_{t_0}$ and produces
$r=g_{\theta_g}(\phi(x;p_{t_0},\theta_{\rm ptm}))$. DRM proposes the task of
$\arg\max r$ and evaluates its adapter when it differs from $t_0$; our fusion
modifies this proposal, not the preceding bare TII pass. CRM selects
low-confidence samples from $r$ by the baseline's generalised-entropy threshold
and compares the current logits, under the baseline log-sum-exp score, with the
adapter of the second-ranked class of $r$; it is confidence-triggered, not
restricted to cases where the two task identities agree. Both reuse trained
modules, can require further adapter forwards, and are heuristics: neither
guarantees that accuracy cannot decrease.

\subsubsection{Shared classification head.}
The selected parameter set is attached to the backbone, and the shared head
produces a score vector over all classes seen so far,
$\ell = g(\phi(x; p_{\hat{t}}, \theta_{\mathrm{ptm}}); \theta_g)
\in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{Y}|}$, predicting $\hat{y} = \arg\max_c \ell_c$, where
$\hat{t}$ is the identity after DRM and CRM. Classes not yet seen are masked
with $-\infty$.

\subsubsection{Two weaknesses this paper acts on.}
The baseline draws its routing evidence from TII and the shared head alone. To
see what a second source would be worth we built the head of
Sect.~\ref{sec:rp} on HRM-PET's own LoRA features and used it as a router: at
seed 42 on ImageNet-R, TII routes $63.80\%$ of samples correctly, the DRM
proposal $77.79\%$ and the RP head $77.12\%$, while the union of TII and the
head reaches $82.99\%$. Since TII is wrong on $36.20\%$ of the set, the head
repairs $53.0\%$ of its errors, against $62.0\%$ and $46.9\%$ on CIFAR-100
and CUB-200. The union is an oracle that no router we build attains, and it
compares bare TII with RP rather than the actual Stage 1 inputs.

A better route need not become a better prediction: $\ell$ comes from the shared
head, which spans every class seen so far, so a mis-routed sample is often
classified correctly anyway. The RP head is moreover a complete classifier that
a routing-only variant would discard: on ImageNet-R the routed head reaches
$74.31\%$ and the projection head $70.08\%$, yet their union is $78.89\%$, the
head alone contributing $4.58$ points of the test set and $2.43$ and $3.87$ on
the other two. These measurements give the two components described next.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem Statement}

Class-incremental learning (CIL) learns a sequence of $T$ tasks without
forgetting earlier ones. The $t$-th task consists of $n_t$ samples
$\mathcal{D}_t=\{(x^t_i,y^t_i)\}_{i=1}^{n_t}$ drawn from a class set
$\mathcal{C}_t$, where $x^t_i \in \mathcal{X}$ is an input image and
$y^t_i \in \mathcal{Y}$ its label.

Three constraints define the setting. At step $t$ only $\mathcal{D}_t$ is
available for training. The class sets are disjoint,
$\mathcal{C}_t \cap \mathcal{C}_{t'} = \emptyset$ for all $t \neq t'$, and at
inference the \emph{task identity is unknown}: the model must discriminate among
all classes seen so far through a single shared classification head. Finally,
this work operates in the exemplar-free setting: images from past tasks are
never retained.

In methods based on parameter-efficient tuning
(PET)~\cite{ref_l2p,ref_dualprompt,ref_hide}, a pre-trained backbone
$h_{\mathrm{ptm}}$ with parameters $\theta_{\mathrm{ptm}}$ is frozen and a
lightweight parameter pool $\mathcal{P}=\{p_1,\dots,p_T\}$ is maintained, each
set trained on one task; here every $p_t$ is a LoRA adapter~\cite{ref_lora}. The
shared classification head is denoted $g_{\theta_g}$. Because the task identity
is unknown at inference, the system is forced to \emph{choose} which parameter
set to attach to the backbone before classifying, and everything downstream
depends on that choice.

We distinguish four class-score vectors: bare TII scores $u$, initial adapted
shared-head logits $r$, final re-matched logits $\ell$, and RP scores $s$.
The class-to-task map is $\mathcal T(\cdot)$; $t_0$ is the initial TII match and
$\hat t_{\rm DRM}$ the task proposed for direct re-matching. We write
$z(\cdot)$ for per-sample standardisation with the population standard
deviation floored at $10^{-6}$.

\subsection{The Second Source of Evidence}
\label{sec:rp}

We fit an RP head after each task and reuse its scores in two inference-time
fusion operations. HRM-PET parameters are not retrained.
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} distinguishes the initial adapted logits from the
bare TII scores and shows both reuse paths.

The RP head follows RanPAC~\cite{ref_ranpac}. Features $f\in\mathbb R^d$,
$d=768$, are column vectors from ViT-B/16 with the first task's LoRA adapter
($p_1$ here, index $0$ in code), fixed during accumulation and inference and
unnormalised; changing adapters between tasks would mix incompatible spaces.

\subsubsection{Projection and second-order statistics.}
The feature is projected into a high-dimensional space of size $M = 10^4$ and
passed through a nonlinearity, and the projected features are accumulated into
two matrices:
\begin{align}
h &= \mathrm{ReLU}(W^\top f) \in \mathbb{R}^{M},
\qquad W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times M},\quad W_{jk}\sim\mathcal N(0,1),
\label{eq:proj}\\
G &= \sum_n h_n h_n^{\top} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times M},
\qquad
C = \sum_n h_n\, \mathbf{e}_{y_n}^{\top}
   \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times |\mathcal{Y}|},
\label{eq:gram}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{e}_{y}$ is the basis vector of class $y$. Each current-task
training sample contributes once; $G$ and $C$ are accumulated in float64.
$W$ is generated once with seed $1993$ and then frozen.

The nonlinear expansion permits richer decision functions than a linear head
on $f$. Without ReLU, $W^\top f$ remains linear in $f$ and adds no separating
power. This does not guarantee that the expanded data become linearly separable.

\subsubsection{Classification by ridge regression.}
The output weights are the closed-form solution of a regularised least-squares
problem:
\begin{equation}
W_{\mathrm{out}} = (G + \lambda I)^{-1} C \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times |\mathcal{Y}|},
\qquad
s = W_{\mathrm{out}}^{\top} h \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{Y}|} .
\label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}
The implementation solves the regularised system rather than inverting it.

\subsubsection{Two inherited properties.}
Both $G$ and $C$ in (\ref{eq:gram}) are \emph{sums} over samples, so for a
\emph{fixed} feature map they, and with them $W_{\mathrm{out}}$, do not depend
on the order in which samples arrived: nothing an earlier task contributed is
overwritten, and the head after $T$ tasks equals the ridge solution of a joint
fit over every feature seen. Their sizes do not grow with the sample count and
no per-sample feature is retained, so the exemplar-free protocol follows from
the structure of the computation. This is weaker than an absence of forgetting
and we claim no more: adding a task adds classes competing for the same arg max,
so accuracy on old classes can fall while their statistics sit untouched, and
the feature map is not fixed across orderings, since features come from the
first task's adapter. The property rules out one mechanism of forgetting, not
the phenomenon.

\subsection{Two-Stage Fusion}

Both stages blend vectors on different scales: the ridge scores $s$ span roughly
one unit and the logits $\ell$ roughly thirty-five, so a direct blend would let
$\ell$ dominate at every weight. Each source is standardised per sample:
\begin{equation}
z(v) = \frac{v - \mathrm{mean}(v)}{\sigma_\epsilon(v)},
\qquad \sigma_\epsilon(v)=\max\{\mathrm{std}(v),10^{-6}\},
\label{eq:znorm}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{std}$ is the population standard deviation. Moments are
over seen tasks in Stage 1 and seen classes in Stage 2, excluding unseen entries.

\subsubsection{First stage: fusion at the routing level.}
Stage 1 runs after the initial adapted pass and before DRM. Its input is $r$,
the shared-head logits under the adapter selected by bare TII, not auxiliary
logits $u$. Both $r$ and RP scores $s$ are mapped to seen tasks by
$r^{\mathrm{task}}_t = \max_{c \in \mathcal{C}_t} r_c$ and
$s^{\mathrm{task}}_t = \max_{c \in \mathcal{C}_t} s_c$ for
$t = 1,\dots,\hat{T}$, with $\hat{T}$ the number of tasks seen so far; classes
of unseen tasks never enter either maximum. The two vectors are blended after standardisation:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{fused} = w\, z(r^{\mathrm{task}}) + (1-w)\, z(s^{\mathrm{task}}),
\qquad
\hat{t}_{\rm DRM} = \arg\max_t\, \mathrm{fused}_t ,
\qquad w = 0.7 .
\label{eq:route}
\end{equation}
For one seen task the standard deviation is zero, so the floor keeps the
operation finite. The fused task replaces the baseline DRM proposal
$\mathcal T(\arg\max r)$; the initial TII match, CRM candidate rule, confidence
test and shared head are unchanged. At $w=1$ the ordering is that of
$r^{\rm task}$, recovering that proposal; the full baseline also needs $\beta=0$.

\subsubsection{Second stage: gated fusion at the classification level.}
Because $\ell$ comes from the shared head, it frequently predicts the correct
class in spite of a wrong routing decision, so we use the projection head itself
as a secondary classifier, this time taking its class scores $s$. A fixed blend
would alter confident primary predictions; we instead reduce the RP share as the
top-two probability margin grows, while recognising that confident predictions
can still be wrong:
\begin{equation}
\beta_i = \beta \left( 1 - \frac{p_{(1)} - p_{(2)}}{p_{(1)} + p_{(2)}} \right),
\qquad \beta = 0.5 ,
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
where $p_{(1)} \geq p_{(2)}$ are the two largest probabilities of
$\mathrm{softmax}(\ell)$ over the classes seen so far. Dividing by the sum makes
the quantity depend only on the ratio $p_{(2)}/p_{(1)}$, so the gate measures how
threatened the top class is rather than how much mass the pair holds. The RP share falls to zero as the top
class dominates and reaches $\beta$ when the two are equal: a continuous weight,
not a binary test.

The blend reuses (\ref{eq:znorm}) and then maps the result back onto the scale of
$\ell$:
\begin{align}
m_i &= (1 - \beta_i)\, z(\ell_i) + \beta_i\, z(s_i),
\label{eq:mix}\\
\hat{\ell}_i &= m_i \cdot \sigma_\epsilon(\ell_i) + \mathrm{mean}(\ell_i)
   \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{Y}|}.
\label{eq:affine}
\end{align}
The positive affine map-back preserves rankings and restores a reference scale
for cross-entropy, not a calibration guarantee: for standardised nondegenerate
vectors with correlation $\rho$ and the floor inactive, the mixture variance is
$(1-\beta_i)^2+\beta_i^2+2\beta_i(1-\beta_i)\rho$, which need not equal one. At
$\beta=0$ this stage leaves $\ell$ unchanged.

\subsubsection{Cost.}
At inference the head costs one extra adapter forward per sample, serving both
stages. That is the head's own cost, not the pipeline's difference, which we did
not measure: changing the DRM proposal can change the number of adapter
evaluations.

Per task the head accumulates $G$ and $C$ over the current task's training split
and solves one ridge system, so its footprint is set by $M$, not by how many
samples have been seen --- and it is not small. At $M = 10^4$ the Gram matrix
holds $10^8$ entries, $800$~MB in the float64 the solve requires, and the
Cholesky factor with the regularised copy brings the peak near $2.4$~GB. A
complete ten-task evaluation on ImageNet-R takes $283$~s on one RTX 4090 at
batch $24$, a total we did not instrument by phase.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

The backbone is a ViT-B/16 pre-trained on ImageNet-21K and adapted with LoRA of
rank $8$. The four-seed study uses Split-ImageNet-R, Split-CIFAR-100 and
Split-CUB-200 in $10$ tasks each; the grid of Sect.~\ref{sec:grid} swaps CUB-200
for Split-ImageNet-A and 5-Datasets, a $5$-task sequence.
The five pre-training settings are Sup-21K, MoCo-v3, iBOT-1K, iBOT-21K and DINO.
Seeds are $42$ to $45$, with the LoRA pool and the TII module retrained from
scratch for each; baseline and proposed method are then evaluated from the same
checkpoints through the same code path. The projection head uses $M = 10^4$, a
ReLU nonlinearity and $\lambda = 10^4$, with no temperature scaling; fusion
starts at task 1; the baseline CRM settings are preserved (\texttt{En=gen},
threshold $-10$, two class-derived candidate tasks).

Acc@task in the released evaluator measures the DRM task proposal. In the
default class-based routing path, CRM can change the output logits without
updating the task identity passed to this metric; it is therefore not a measure
of final adapter selection after CRM. Acc@1, Acc@5 and Loss use the final
classification logits. Writing $a_{i,t}$ for classification accuracy on task $i$
at stage $t$, the retention measures are
\begin{align}
\mathrm{Forgetting}
 &= \frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{i=1}^{T-1}
 \left(\max_{i\le t\le T} a_{i,t}-a_{i,T}\right),\\
\mathrm{Backward}
 &= \frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{i=1}^{T-1}(a_{i,T}-a_{i,i}).
\label{eq:metrics}
\end{align}
Both averages exclude the last task, and the maximum includes only stages at
which task $i$ has been learned. Lower is better for Loss and Forgetting.

\subsubsection{Sanity check.}
$w = 1.0$ in (\ref{eq:route}) restores the original DRM proposal and
$\beta = 0$ in (\ref{eq:mix}) leaves $\hat{\ell} = \ell$; with both set the
method reproduces the HRM-PET baseline digit for digit on all three datasets ---
$77.7914$, $74.0191$, $1.2305$, the rest agreeing to the fourth decimal --- so
every difference in Table~\ref{tab:main} comes from the added components, not
the evaluation path.

The gain is $+1.10$ to $+2.26$ points, with positive paired intervals on all

\subsubsection{Classification (Acc@1, Acc@5): improved.}
Acc@1 rises by $+0.69$ to $+1.50$ points and Acc@5 by $+0.22$ to $+1.31$,

gains do not establish how many routing repairs also repair classification.

\subsubsection{Retention (Forgetting, Backward): small, and mostly unresolved.}
Read as paired differences over the four seeds, the $95\%$ interval from $t_3$
covers zero in five of the six retention cells; the exception, Forgetting on
CIFAR-100, is $-0.156$ with interval $[-0.312, -0.000]$, on the boundary. The
two metrics are near mirror images --- $-0.156$ against $+0.142$ on CIFAR-100,
$-0.359$ against $+0.279$ on CUB-200 --- and are not independent replications.
We claim no consistent improvement here, and no equivalence either: four seeds
give intervals wide enough to hold differences that would matter, and we fixed
no margin in advance. The single-seed decomposition in Sect.~\ref{sec:grid} is
descriptive: final accuracy on earlier tasks rises in every grid cell, while
Backward changes little because accuracy at learning time also rises.

\subsection{The Full Grid}
\label{sec:grid}

The original work covers four datasets by five pre-training configurations; we
rebuilt all twenty cells, evaluating both methods through one code path at seed
$42$. Every cell improves on both accuracy axes: Acc@task by $+0.97$ to $+4.57$
and Acc@1 by $+0.59$ to $+3.58$, against baselines spanning $31.8$ to $93.8$
Acc@1. Retention is descriptive rather than conclusive: final accuracy on
earlier tasks rises in all twenty cells, by $+1.83$ on average, while accuracy
at learning time rises by $+1.58$, so Backward, their difference, stays small.
It worsens in five cells, which by that identity means learning-time accuracy
rose more there --- a widening gap, not a fall in final accuracy.

\subsection{Ablation}
\label{sec:ablation}

We ablate \emph{cumulatively}, as the original work does, on ImageNet-R at seed
$42$ across its five pre-training configurations, whose baselines run from
$68.47$ to $74.02$ Acc@1. Only differences are reported, since only they carry
the argument.

\subsubsection{Routing gains depend on the gate.}
Each stage alone beats the baseline on every backbone --- routing by $+0.29$ to
$+0.74$, the classifier by $+0.51$ to $+3.07$ --- yet they are far from additive.
With the gate off, adding routing to class fusion moves Acc@1 by only $-0.07$ to
$+0.02$; that near-zero net does not establish identical predictions, since
repairs and new errors may cancel.

With the gate on, routing adds $+0.13$ to $+0.77$, positive on all five
backbones, while the gate changes Acc@1 by $-1.32$ to $+0.85$ with class-only
fusion against $-0.54$ to $+0.96$ when routing is present. This is an
interaction: re-matching may change the confidence the gate acts on, though
aggregate accuracies again do not say which predictions move.

A single seed cannot settle a difference of a few tenths, so we repeated the
comparison that matters --- the full method against ungated class-only fusion
($w = 1$, $\beta = 0.5$, no gate) --- over seeds 42--45. Class-only reaches
$74.49\pm0.14$, $90.15\pm0.16$ and $88.01\pm0.21$ Acc@1 on ImageNet-R, CIFAR-100
and CUB-200, against $75.18\pm0.24$, $90.47\pm0.05$ and $87.88\pm0.27$ for the
full method: paired changes of $+0.69$ $[+0.36, +1.01]$ and $+0.33$
$[+0.03, +0.62]$, whose intervals exclude zero, and $-0.13$ $[-0.48, +0.23]$,
which is unresolved and does not establish equivalence. The comparison cannot
isolate routing, since it also changes the gate; the routing effect conditional
on gating is supported only by the preceding single-seed, five-backbone
ablation.

The first stage is also the only component that moves Acc@task, though the two
Acc@1 contributions must not be added. The classifier's isolated contribution is
smallest by far on Sup-21K, $+0.51$ against $+2.23$ to $+3.07$ elsewhere --- the
backbone every design decision was taken on.

\label{sec:weights}

$\beta$ afterwards, so the second stage may have shifted the optimum of the

Backward. That comparison predates the second stage, so it does not transfer to
the full method; separating the two values there would need the same four seeds
at fixed $\beta$ and gate, which we have not run.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

Design choices were explored on ImageNet-R, and CUB-200 also carries development
experiments, so neither is an untouched holdout; CIFAR-100 tests transfer, but
more seeds do not remove selection bias. The four-seed comparison changes
routing and gating jointly, so a class-plus-gate control is still needed to
isolate routing, and the twenty-cell grid, the conditional routing ablation and
the weight grid are single-seed. Rows quoted in Table~\ref{tab:prior} come from
another implementation, and on 5-Datasets our result is below the published
figure. We claim no consistent retention benefit or non-inferiority, did not
split runtime into its phases, and did not measure repair/harm counts.

\section{Conclusion}

A fixed-feature RP classifier complements HRM-PET at two decisions, the DRM task
proposal and final classification, at one extra adapter forward and a ridge fit
per task. Acc@1 rises by $0.69$--$1.50$ points over three datasets and four
seeds, and in all twenty cells of a single-seed pre-training grid; the four-seed
comparison supports adding routing and gating jointly on two of three. The gains
do not establish a reduction in forgetting.
