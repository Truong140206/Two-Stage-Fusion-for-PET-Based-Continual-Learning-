\begin{abstract}
Task-specific parameter-efficient continual learners must select adapters
without knowing the task identity, and better routing need not yield better
classification. We propose two-stage score fusion using a random-projection
(RP) head fitted by ridge regression over fixed first-task LoRA features.
Its statistics accumulate across tasks without retaining examples or changing
task-specific adapter training. The same RP scores serve two decision points:
task-proposal fusion before re-matching and margin-gated class fusion afterwards.
Across three benchmarks and four seeds, the method improves DRM-proposal
accuracy by $1.10$--$2.26$ percentage points and classification accuracy by
$0.69$--$1.50$. A single-seed grid across four datasets and five pre-training
settings shows classification gains in all twenty comparisons. Matched-head
controls and conditional ablations distinguish the RP classifier's contribution
from the effects of routing and gating. These effects depend on the dataset
and fusion configuration; neither component is uniformly beneficial.
The results support improved prediction, not a consistent reduction in
forgetting or backward-transfer loss.

\keywords{Continual learning \and Parameter-efficient tuning \and Parameter
routing \and Random projection \and Signal fusion.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Continual learning asks a model to absorb a stream of tasks without losing what
earlier ones taught it. Parameter-efficient tuning adapts a frozen pre-trained
backbone using a small number of parameters. One important family maintains
task-specific prompts or adapters and selects them at
inference~\cite{ref_dualprompt,ref_hide}. Such methods can avoid storing old
examples, but preserving parameters does not guarantee preserved accuracy.

In this family, task identity is unknown at inference and parameter selection
is itself a prediction. A wrong adapter can produce unsuitable features.
Larger pools, attention-weighted mixtures and explicit re-matching offer
different responses to this difficulty~\cite{ref_l2p,ref_coda,ref_hrmpet}.
Yet selecting a more suitable adapter and predicting the correct class are
different objectives: a shared classifier can recover from a routing error,
while a routing change can also disturb an already correct prediction.

We add a complementary predictor: first-task adapter features, a frozen random
projection and a ridge classifier fitted from accumulated statistics, following
RanPAC~\cite{ref_ranpac}. Fitting uses current-task data without changing LoRA/TII
training or retaining examples. Its scores revise the task proposal before
re-matching and blend with final class logits, with greater weight when the
primary classifier's top-two margin narrows.

\subsubsection{Contributions.}
We contribute (i) a shared RP predictor for task-proposal and margin-gated class
fusion, (ii) matched-feature controls and four-seed conditional ablations, and
(iii) a four-dataset, five-pre-training study with weight sensitivity.
The novelty is the two-point integration and its evaluation, not ridge learning.
We distinguish classification gains from retention and oracle complementarity
from achieved corrections.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsubsection{Parameter pools and the selection they require.}
L2P~\cite{ref_l2p} and DualPrompt~\cite{ref_dualprompt} use queries from a frozen
backbone to select lightweight parameters from a pool.
CODA-Prompt~\cite{ref_coda} combines prompts using attention weights.
HiDe-Prompt~\cite{ref_hide} separates within-task prediction, task-identity
inference and task-adaptive prediction, identifying identity inference as a
bottleneck. HRM-PET~\cite{ref_hrmpet} uses LoRA adapters~\cite{ref_lora} and
re-matching to revisit initial selections. MoTE~\cite{ref_mote} combines
task-specific experts; DLEPEM~\cite{ref_dlepem} combines general and adapted
prototypes for matching.

\subsubsection{Closed-form solutions in continual learning.}
Closed-form classifiers preserve past contributions through additive statistics
of fixed features. APER~\cite{ref_adam} adapts the backbone on the first task,
then uses frozen features and class prototypes. ACIL~\cite{ref_acil} uses
recursive least squares, replacing a sample buffer with correlation statistics
and recovering the corresponding joint solution. DS-AL~\cite{ref_dsal} adds a
second analytic stream. RanPAC~\cite{ref_ranpac} applies a fixed random
projection to frozen features, accumulates second-order statistics and solves
a ridge system.

We inherit RanPAC's projection, Gram accumulation and ridge solve, but retain
the adapter-routing pipeline and consult the head at two decision points
instead of using it as the sole predictor.

\subsubsection{Correcting the classifier after the fact.}
Class-incremental learning often biases predictions towards recent
classes~\cite{ref_survey}. iCaRL~\cite{ref_icarl} uses nearest exemplar means;
BiC~\cite{ref_bic} learns an affine logit correction on held-out data;
Weight Aligning~\cite{ref_wa} matches new- and old-class weight norms; and
LUCIR~\cite{ref_lucir} normalises features and weights. Our fusion leaves the
adapters, auxiliary classifier and shared head fixed. It retains no exemplars
but requires current-task data to fit the RP head.

\subsubsection{Remaining challenge.}
Prompt/classifier inconsistency between training and testing can degrade
prediction~\cite{ref_cprompt}; re-matching addresses parameter selection but
does not supply an independent class predictor. We adopt the re-matching
interface of HRM-PET~\cite{ref_hrmpet}, retaining its adapter and classifier
training, and test complementary RP evidence before and after re-matching.
This differs from replacing adapter selection with an analytic classifier.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem Statement}

Class-incremental learning (CIL) aims to learn $T$ tasks while mitigating
forgetting. Task $t$ provides labelled images
$\mathcal D_t=\{(x_i^t,y_i^t)\}_{i=1}^{n_t}$ from class set $\mathcal C_t$,
with $\mathcal C_t\cap\mathcal C_{t'}=\emptyset$ for $t\ne t'$.
Only current-task data are available for training; past images are never
retained. At inference task identity is unknown and prediction spans all seen
classes.

Our task-specific PET setting~\cite{ref_hide,ref_hrmpet} freezes backbone
parameters $\theta_{\mathrm{ptm}}$ and maintains LoRA adapters
$\mathcal P=\{p_1,\dots,p_T\}$, one per task, with shared classification head
$g_{\theta_g}$. The primary prediction requires adapter selection; our fixed
RP branch is independent of the inferred task.

We distinguish four class-score vectors: bare TII scores $u$, initial adapted
shared-head logits $r$, final re-matched logits $\ell$, and RP scores $s$.
The class-to-task map is $\mathcal T(\cdot)$. The auxiliary head $g_\omega$
maps bare features to $u$, giving $t_0=\mathcal T(\arg\max_c u_c)$.
The initial adapted pass gives $r=g(\phi(x;p_{t_0}))$.
DRM evaluates its proposed adapter only if $\hat t_{\rm DRM}\ne t_0$.
CRM uses the generalised-entropy confidence of $r$, tries the task of its
second-ranked class, and retains logits according to log-sum-exp; it is not
restricted to agreement between task proposals and does not run at task 1.
All adapted passes share $g$; $\ell$ denotes the retained class logits.
We write
$z(\cdot)$ for per-sample standardisation with the population standard
deviation floored at $10^{-6}$.

\subsection{The Second Source of Evidence}
\label{sec:rp}

We fit an RP head after each task and reuse its scores in two inference-time
fusion operations. The existing PET modules receive no additional gradient updates.
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} distinguishes the initial adapted logits from the
bare TII scores and shows both reuse paths.

The RP head follows RanPAC~\cite{ref_ranpac}. Features $f\in\mathbb R^d$,
$d=768$, are column vectors from ViT-B/16 with the first task's LoRA adapter
($p_1$ here, index $0$ in code), fixed during accumulation and inference and
unnormalised; changing adapters between tasks would mix incompatible spaces.

\subsubsection{Projection and second-order statistics.}
We expand $f$ to $M=10^4$ dimensions and retain two accumulated matrices:
\begin{align}
h &= \mathrm{ReLU}(W^\top f) \in \mathbb{R}^{M},
\qquad W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times M},\quad W_{jk}\sim\mathcal N(0,1),
\label{eq:proj}\\
G &= \sum_n h_n h_n^{\top} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times M},
\qquad
C = \sum_n h_n\, \mathbf{e}_{y_n}^{\top}
   \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times |\mathcal{Y}|},
\label{eq:gram}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{e}_{y}$ is the basis vector of class $y$. Each current-task
training sample contributes once; $G$ and $C$ are accumulated in float64.
$W$ is generated once with seed $1993$ and then frozen.

The nonlinear expansion permits richer decision functions than a linear head
on $f$. Without ReLU, $W^\top f$ remains linear in $f$ and adds no separating
power. This does not guarantee that the expanded data become linearly separable.

\subsubsection{Classification by ridge regression.}
The output weights are the closed-form solution of a regularised least-squares
problem:
\begin{equation}
W_{\mathrm{out}} = (G + \lambda I)^{-1} C \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times |\mathcal{Y}|},
\qquad
s = W_{\mathrm{out}}^{\top} h \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{Y}|} .
\label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}
The implementation solves the regularised system rather than inverting it.

\subsubsection{Two inherited properties.}
For a fixed feature map, the sums in (\ref{eq:gram}) preserve earlier
contributions and yield the joint ridge solution over all observed features,
independent of sample order in exact arithmetic. Storage is independent of
sample count and retains no per-sample features. This does not imply bitwise
floating-point invariance or zero forgetting: new classes can outscore old
ones. Changing the first task also changes its adapter and thus the feature map.

\subsection{Two-Stage Fusion}

Both stages blend sources with different score scales. Direct mixing can make
the nominal weights poorly reflect their influence. Each source is standardised per sample:
\begin{equation}
z(v) = \frac{v - \mathrm{mean}(v)}{\sigma_\epsilon(v)},
\qquad \sigma_\epsilon(v)=\max\{\mathrm{std}(v),10^{-6}\},
\label{eq:znorm}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{std}$ is the population standard deviation. Moments are
over seen tasks in Stage 1 and seen classes in Stage 2, excluding unseen entries.

\subsubsection{First stage: fusion at the routing level.}
Stage 1 runs after the initial adapted pass and before DRM. Its input is $r$,
the shared-head logits under the adapter selected by bare TII, not auxiliary
logits $u$. Both $r$ and RP scores $s$ are mapped to seen tasks by
$r^{\mathrm{task}}_t = \max_{c \in \mathcal{C}_t} r_c$ and
$s^{\mathrm{task}}_t = \max_{c \in \mathcal{C}_t} s_c$ for
$t = 1,\dots,\hat{T}$, with $\hat{T}$ the number of tasks seen so far; classes
of unseen tasks never enter either maximum. The two vectors are blended after standardisation:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{fused} = w\, z(r^{\mathrm{task}}) + (1-w)\, z(s^{\mathrm{task}}),
\qquad
\hat{t}_{\rm DRM} = \arg\max_t\, \mathrm{fused}_t .
\label{eq:route}
\end{equation}
For one seen task the standard deviation is zero, so the floor keeps the
operation finite. The fused task replaces the baseline DRM proposal
$\mathcal T(\arg\max r)$; the initial TII match, CRM candidate rule, confidence
test and shared head are unchanged. At $w=1$ the ordering is that of
$r^{\rm task}$, recovering that proposal; the full baseline also needs $\beta=0$.

\subsubsection{Second stage: gated fusion at the classification level.}
Because $\ell$ comes from the shared head, it can predict the correct
class in spite of a wrong routing decision, so we use the projection head itself
as a secondary classifier, this time taking its class scores $s$. A fixed blend
would alter confident primary predictions; we instead reduce the RP share as the
top-two probability margin grows, while recognising that confident predictions
can still be wrong:
\begin{equation}
\beta_i = \beta \left( 1 - \frac{p_{(1)} - p_{(2)}}{p_{(1)} + p_{(2)}} \right),
\qquad \beta = 0.5 ,
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
where $p_{(1)} \geq p_{(2)}$ are the two largest probabilities of
$\mathrm{softmax}(\ell)$ over the classes seen so far. Dividing by the sum makes
the quantity depend only on the ratio $p_{(2)}/p_{(1)}$, so the gate measures how
threatened the top class is rather than how much mass the pair holds. The RP share falls to zero as the top
class dominates and reaches $\beta$ when the two are equal: a continuous weight,
not a binary test.

The blend reuses (\ref{eq:znorm}) and then maps the result back onto the scale of
$\ell$:
\begin{align}
m_i &= (1 - \beta_i)\, z(\ell_i) + \beta_i\, z(s_i),
\label{eq:mix}\\
\hat{\ell}_i &= m_i \cdot \sigma_\epsilon(\ell_i) + \mathrm{mean}(\ell_i)
   \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{Y}|}.
\label{eq:affine}
\end{align}
The positive affine map-back preserves rankings and restores a reference scale
for cross-entropy, not a calibration guarantee: for standardised nondegenerate
vectors with correlation $\rho$ and the floor inactive, the mixture variance is
$(1-\beta_i)^2+\beta_i^2+2\beta_i(1-\beta_i)\rho$, which need not equal one. At
$\beta=0$ this stage leaves $\ell$ unchanged.

\subsubsection{Cost.}
The head adds one adapter forward per sample, shared by both fusion stages.
This is not measured total overhead: changing the DRM proposal can also change
the number of adapter evaluations.

Per task the head accumulates $G$ and $C$ over the current task's training split
and solves one ridge system. Its stored arrays scale as
$O(M^2+M|\mathcal Y|+dM)$: $G$ has $M^2$ entries, $C$ and $W_{\rm out}$
each have $M|\mathcal Y|$, and the projection has $dM$. Storage does not grow
with the number of observed samples for fixed dimensions and class capacity.
The Gram matrix dominates in our setting. At $M = 10^4$ it holds $10^8$ float64
entries, or $800$~MB. Together with its regularised copy and Cholesky factor,
these three matrices require approximately $2.4$~GB; this is an analytical
estimate, not a measured total-memory peak. A historical ten-task evaluation
on ImageNet-R took $283$~s on one RTX 4090 at batch $24$; neither its
phase-level costs nor its overhead over the baseline were measured.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

The backbone is ViT-B/16~\cite{ref_vit}, pre-trained on ImageNet-21K.
Rank-$8$ LoRA modifies the key/value projections in the first five blocks.
The four-seed study splits ImageNet-R~\cite{ref_imr}, CIFAR-100~\cite{ref_cifar}
and CUB-200-2011~\cite{ref_cub} into $10$ tasks of $20$, $10$ and $20$ classes,
respectively. The grid replaces CUB with ImageNet-A~\cite{ref_ima} ($10\times20$
classes) and the five-dataset protocol of~\cite{ref_hrmpet} (one domain per task).
Its pre-training settings are Sup-21K, MoCo-v3~\cite{ref_moco},
iBOT-1K/iBOT-21K~\cite{ref_ibot} and DINO~\cite{ref_dino}.

Each seed (42--45) has separately trained LoRA/TII parameters; pre-trained
backbone weights remain frozen. Both methods use identical saved checkpoints
and evaluation paths. RP fits current-task training images once using the
evaluation transform: resize to $256$, centre crop to $224$, and convert to a
tensor; CIFAR additionally uses its configured channel normalisation. No
test labels enter the fit. We use $M=10^4$, ReLU, $\lambda=10^4$, no additional
RP feature normalisation or temperature calibration, and no fusion ramp.
Unless noted otherwise, we report $w=0.7$ and $\beta=0.5$ throughout,
not a dataset-wise best configuration. The joint sensitivity study in
Sect.~\ref{sec:weights} includes the better observed $w=0.6$ setting; we do
not retroactively select the main configuration on that test grid.
Fusion starts at task 1; CRM retains \texttt{En=gen}, threshold $-10$, and the
second-ranked class candidate. Training/evaluation scripts and dataset
preprocessing are versioned in the code repository~\cite{ref_code}.

Acc@task in the released evaluator measures the DRM task proposal. In the
default class-based routing path, CRM can change the output logits without
updating the task identity passed to this metric; it is therefore not a measure
of final adapter selection after CRM. Acc@1, Acc@5 and Loss use the final
classification logits. Final metrics average the per-task values with equal
task weights, not by pooling all test images. Writing $a_{i,t}$ for classification accuracy on task $i$
at stage $t$, the retention measures are
\begin{align}
\mathrm{Forgetting}
 &= \frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{i=1}^{T-1}
 \left(\max_{i\le t\le T} a_{i,t}-a_{i,T}\right),\\
\mathrm{Backward}
 &= \frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{i=1}^{T-1}(a_{i,T}-a_{i,i}).
\label{eq:metrics}
\end{align}
Both averages exclude the last task, and the maximum includes only stages at
which task $i$ has been learned. Lower is better for Loss and Forgetting;
higher is better for accuracy and Backward (including when Backward is negative).

\subsubsection{Reproducibility and sanity check.}
With corrected unseen-class masking, $w=1,\beta=0$ recovers the unfused path
to logged precision at every stage for all three datasets and seeds 42--45.
For example, both paths give ImageNet-R seed-42 Acc@1 $74.0191$.
This identity does not imply harmless intermediate fusion settings.
Main runs, controls, grids, ablations and diagnostics were re-evaluated;
corrected loss/retention values are used throughout. All forty grid evaluations
and fixed backbone/first-adapter tensors were checked.

\subsection{Complementarity Diagnostics}
\label{sec:diagnostics}

These seed-$42$ diagnostics use equal task weights (Sect.~\ref{sec:setup}).
On ImageNet-R, bare TII routing accuracy is $63.80\%$, the baseline DRM
proposal $77.79\%$ and the RP router $77.12\%$. The TII--RP oracle union reaches
$82.99\%$. Dividing the RP-correct/TII-wrong rate by TII's error rate gives
$53.0\%$, versus $62.0\%$ on CIFAR-100 and $46.9\%$ on CUB-200.
These are task-balanced recovery ratios, not pooled image counts or achieved
fusion accuracy. They compare bare TII with RP, not Stage 1's adapted logits
with RP.

Complementarity also exists at the class level. In the routing-only diagnostic
($w=0.7$, $\beta=0$), ImageNet-R accuracy is $74.31\%$ for the routed head and
$70.08\%$ for RP, with an oracle union of $78.89\%$. RP is correct when the
routed head is wrong on $4.58$ task-balanced percentage points, versus $2.43$
on CIFAR-100 and $3.87$ on CUB-200. This potential motivates reusing $s$ for
classification, but does not count actual repairs or harms of full fusion.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

ImageNet-R and CUB informed development; neither is an untouched holdout.
More seeds do not remove selection bias. CUB/pre-training overlap~\cite{ref_cub}
was not audited. The grids and five-backbone ablation are single-seed; only
the three-dataset contrasts use four seeds. CIFAR's conditional routing effect
is unresolved, and gate benefits vary across backbones.
The additional RanPAC comparison matches pretrained weights, images and class
order only for one run; training budgets and runtimes differ, and multi-order
verification is incomplete. The RP-only control in Table~\ref{tab:components}
is not a full RanPAC reproduction. Quoted baselines use another implementation;
our Sup-21K 5-Datasets result is below the published figure. Retention benefit/equivalence remains
unestablished; phase-level cost and actual full-fusion repair/harm counts
were not measured.
The loader skips two zero-byte NotMNIST training files, also empty in the
local source archive; we did not replace them.

\section{Conclusion}

Reusing one fixed-feature RP predictor for task proposals and class scores
improves classification by $0.69$--$1.50$ Acc@1 points over the matched PET system across three datasets and four
seeds, without changing adapter training or storing past images. Component
comparisons show that class fusion provides much of the gain; the added value
of routing and gating is conditional, not universal. The method improves
prediction but does not establish reduced forgetting. Controlled cost
measurements and independent validation are priorities for further evaluation.
